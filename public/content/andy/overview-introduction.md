## OVERVIEW
Hello! Welcome to the Slayer Legend guide created by me (Andy). This guide is designed to break down how to progress efficiently in the game as a F2P player or a light spender (definitely not 🐳 status). While there are surely extreme min-max strategies out there, everything here comes from my own experience with characters on both Server 1 and Server 2. I've been playing Slayer Legend for about three years now, so I'd like to think I have a solid grasp of how things work. So without further ado, let's dive into progression!

## INTRODUCTION
So, you've created your character, huh? Don't worry, you'll be able to customize your Slayer Legend avatar as you obtain new outfits, and you can change your name once you reach level 100.

From this point on, I'll be skipping ahead to stage 340 (Ether Promotion), which marks the beginning of mid game. Anything before that is considered early game and is generally easy to progress through without much trouble. If you need help during these stages, I recommend checking the main Slayer Legend discord.

## About This Guide - Notes from the Wiki Team

A little context on why this introduction anchors the wiki's strategy section, and where to read next once you've finished it.

### Who It's For

Andy says it himself above, but it bears underlining: this is a guide for **F2P players and light spenders**, written by someone who progressed that way on two separate servers. That focus matters more than it might sound. Plenty of progression advice floating around the community quietly assumes you'll buy your way past bottlenecks - and for a free player, advice like that isn't just unhelpful, it's actively misleading about what to prioritize. Everything here was earned at free-player rates, which means the sequencing survives contact with a free player's actual resource income. If you're a heavy spender you'll still find the structure useful; you'll just hit the milestones faster.

It's also a *mid-game-onward* guide by design. Andy skips ahead to stage 340 (the Ether promotion) because, as he notes, the stretch before it rarely needs a guide. If you're earlier than that, start with the wiki's [Early Game Roadmap](/getting-started/early-game-roadmap) and come back - the pages below pick up exactly where it ends.

### The Philosophy

Efficiency for a free player is mostly a sequencing problem. Diamonds, dice, and emeralds arrive slowly, so *where they go first* matters far more than how many you have - which is why the [Early Game Roadmap](/getting-started/early-game-roadmap) settles diamond priorities before any stage band is discussed, and why progression is organised around the promotion ladder rather than a shopping list. That mirrors the principle the wiki's own [progression overview](/progression) keeps hammering: chase multipliers, let increments fill the gaps. The pages below are that principle worked out in practice, band by band.

### How to Use It

Don't read the wiki front to back. Find the band you're currently in from the table above, read its pages plus the next band ahead, and you'll know both what to do now and what you're saving toward. The guide pages tell you *what to prioritize and when*; for the underlying numbers, keep the reference pages beside them - [promotions](/character/promotions), [stats](/character/stats), [spirits](/spirits/information), the [skill database](/database/skills) - and use the [Skill Builder](/skill-builder) and [Battle Loadouts](/battle-loadouts) tools to sketch the setups each band calls for before you spend anything. Guide for the decisions, references for the data. That combination is the whole wiki in miniature.
