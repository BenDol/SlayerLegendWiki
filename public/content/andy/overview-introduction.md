## OVERVIEW
Hello! Welcome to the Slayer Legend guide created by me (Andy). This guide is designed to break down how to progress efficiently in the game as a F2P player or a light spender (definitely not 🐳 status). While there are surely extreme min-max strategies out there, everything here comes from my own experience with characters on both Server 1 and Server 2. I’ve been playing Slayer Legend for about three years now, so I’d like to think I have a solid grasp of how things work. So without further ado, let’s dive into progression!

## INTRODUCTION
So, you’ve created your character, huh? Don’t worry, you’ll be able to customize your Slayer Legend avatar as you obtain new outfits, and you can change your name once you reach level 100.

From this point on, I'll be skipping ahead to stage 340 (Ether Promotion), which marks the beginning of mid game. Anything before that is considered early game and is generally easy to progress through without much trouble. If you need help during these stages, I recommend checking the main Slayer Legend discord.

## About This Guide - Notes from the Wiki Team

A little context on why this guide anchors the wiki's strategy section, and how to get the most out of it.

### Who It's For

Andy says it himself above, but it bears underlining: this is a guide for **F2P players and light spenders**, written by someone who progressed that way on two separate servers. That focus matters more than it might sound. Plenty of progression advice floating around the community quietly assumes you'll buy your way past bottlenecks - and for a free player, advice like that isn't just unhelpful, it's actively misleading about what to prioritize. Everything here was earned at free-player rates, which means the sequencing survives contact with a free player's actual resource income. If you're a heavy spender you'll still find the structure useful; you'll just hit the milestones faster.

It's also a *mid-game-onward* guide by design. Andy skips ahead to stage 340 (the Ether promotion) because, as he notes, the stretch before it rarely needs a guide. If you're earlier than that, start with the wiki's [Early Game Roadmap](/getting-started/early-game-roadmap) and come back - this guide picks up exactly where it ends.

### The Philosophy

Efficiency for a free player is mostly a sequencing problem. Diamonds, dice, and emeralds arrive slowly, so *where they go first* matters far more than how many you have - which is why the guide gives diamond gain methods their own section before touching a single stage band, and why its structure follows the promotion ladder rather than a shopping list. That mirrors the principle the wiki's own [progression overview](/progression) keeps hammering: chase multipliers, let increments fill the gaps. Andy's guide is that principle worked out in practice, band by band, with three years of receipts.

### How to Use It

Don't read it front to back. The table of contents above maps each section to a stage range and its promotion - find the band you're currently in, read it plus the next one ahead, and you'll know both what to do now and what you're saving toward. The guide tells you *what to prioritize and when*; for the underlying numbers, keep the wiki's reference pages beside it - [promotions](/character/promotions), [stats](/character/stats), [spirits](/spirits/information), the [skill database](/database/skills) - and use the [Skill Builder](/skill-builder) and [Battle Loadouts](/battle-loadouts) tools to sketch the setups each band calls for before you spend anything. Guide for the decisions, references for the data. That combination is the whole wiki in miniature.
