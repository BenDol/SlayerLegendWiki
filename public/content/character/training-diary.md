# <span class="text-gray-900 dark:text-gray-100">Why the Diary Matters</span>

By the time the diary unlocks at level 400, growth points are already one of your core stat sources - every level you gain hands you points to allocate across your [combat and miscellaneous stats](/character/stats). What the diary changes is the *ceiling*. Growth-point stats have level caps, and training with Sepir is what raises them; Over Points then fill the room the raised caps create. In other words, the diary isn't a bonus system sitting off to the side - it is the mechanism that lets your growth-point allocation keep scaling for the rest of the game.

The individual numbers look small. Twenty Over Points per session, three sessions a day - that's easy to shrug at when your ATK stat has six digits. But the diary is the textbook case of a daily system that compounds: sessions take a few minutes, the points are permanent, and a missed day is simply gone. The [farming guide's](/resources/farming) rule applies here in full - daily-limited sources don't stack up, you claim them or lose them. Over months, the gap between a player who never skips the diary and one who does it "when they remember" is a real, visible stat difference, and the diligent player paid almost nothing in time for it.

The dummy trainings deserve their own mention. They're one of the few EXP sources that don't depend on your farming stage at all - flat, guaranteed EXP three times a day (see the [Leveling Guide](/progression/leveling) for where they fit in the wider EXP picture). Post-400, every 100 levels means more Over Points and higher caps, so dummy EXP feeds directly back into the diary loop. It's a tidy little flywheel: train, level, unlock more training.

# <span class="text-gray-900 dark:text-gray-100">Prioritizing Your Over Points</span>

Treat Over Points the way you treat every other stat decision in this game: hedge if you like, but the answer is usually **ATK**. Attack is what pushes stages, pushed stages raise every reward stream, and the rest of the wiki's stat advice - from [first steps](/getting-started/first-steps) through [Andy's guide](/andy/overview-introduction) - points the same direction. The exceptions are the honest ones:

- **Survival walls.** If a [promotion battle](/character/promotions) is killing you rather than out-scaling you, shift points into HP and HP Recovery until it stops. Promotion fights are timed, and staying alive is half the DPS check.
- **Critical Damage** earns its share once your critical stats are developed, since crit damage multiplies with the rest of the critical family rather than adding to it.

You can reset your Over Point allocation for<img src="/images/content/goods/Goods_Diamond.png" alt="" class="inline-image" style="display: inline-block; vertical-align: middle;width: 30px; height: 30px; margin: -5px 0 0 0;" data-inline="true" />**5,000** - noticeably steeper than the 3,000-diamond growth-point reset - so it's worth having a rough plan before you spray points everywhere. Not a detailed spreadsheet plan. Just "ATK unless something specific is wrong" will do.

# <span class="text-gray-900 dark:text-gray-100">Common Mistakes</span>

- **Skipping sessions.** The big one. Three per day, no banking, no catch-up mechanic. Fold the diary into your daily lap alongside dungeon runs and login rewards and it stops being a thing you remember - it's just part of the routine.
- **Leaving Over Points unspent.** Points sitting in your pool do nothing. Unlocked means spend.
- **Forgetting the dummy.** Finishing Sepir's session and closing the menu leaves free EXP on the table. The dummy trainings are separate from the Sepir sessions - check both.
- **Resetting casually.** At 5,000 diamonds a reset, "I'll just re-spec real quick" is an expensive habit. Diamonds do far more work in [weapon summons](/getting-started/early-game-roadmap) than in fixing avoidable allocation mistakes.
