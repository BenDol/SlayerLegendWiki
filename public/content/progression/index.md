# <span class="text-gray-900 dark:text-gray-100">How Progression Works</span>

Slayer Legend's progression is a braid of three strands: your **level** (EXP), your **stage** (how far you've pushed), and your **promotion rank** (the permanent multipliers). They feed each other constantly - higher stages give more EXP and gold, more levels unlock systems and stat points, and promotions multiply you through the next set of stages. When one strand stalls, the other two are how you unstick it. That's the entire strategic rhythm of the game, honestly: identify the lagging strand, feed it, repeat for two thousand stages.

# <span class="text-gray-900 dark:text-gray-100">The Three Phases of the Game</span>

The community splits the 2,000-stage journey into three rough eras, and it's a useful frame because each one has a different bottleneck.

**Early game (stage 1 to the Ether promotion, ~stage 340).** Forgiving by design. Gold into ATK, promotions on time, skills equipped, and the game mostly plays itself - the real work is building habits and not wasting diamonds. The [Early Game Roadmap](/getting-started/early-game-roadmap) walks this whole stretch milestone by milestone, including the class unlock at stage 120 and the training diary at level 400.

**Mid game (Ether through roughly stage 1300).** This is where progression becomes a craft. Each promotion band - Black Mythril, Demon Metal, Dragonos, Ragnablood, and onward - introduces or deepens a system (awakened latent power arrives at Dragonos, soul weapon engraving matures, skill mastery compounds), and the difference between efficient and casual play widens enormously. [Andy's Progression Guide](/andy/overview-introduction) covers this era promotion by promotion, which is exactly how it should be navigated.

**Late game (Gigarock and beyond, stage 1300+).** The multipliers get absurd - the [promotion table](/character/promotions) runs up through Eisenhart, Diadust, and Eldenwood at x42,000,000 ATK and HP - and progress becomes a long-cycle rhythm of feeding every system at once. By this point you don't need an overview page; you need the reference tables and patience.

# <span class="text-gray-900 dark:text-gray-100">When You Hit a Wall</span>

Every player hits stages that simply will not clear, and the diagnosis order is almost always the same. **Check your promotion first** - recommended levels march upward in lockstep with the stages (roughly level 50 for Bronze, 1000 for Ether, 3000 for Gigarock; the [Leveling Guide](/progression/leveling) has the pattern), and a wall very often just means a promotion battle you haven't taken. **Then check the timed-fight essentials**: promotion battles reward survivability and burst, so boss-lineup [spirits](/spirits/information) like Noah, shifted [companion](/companions/companion-builds) levels, and a single-target skill preset can carry an attempt your raw stats couldn't. **Then, and only then, grind** - park at your highest instant-clear stage, stack your buffs, and let the [farming doctrine](/resources/farming) refill the tank. Walls in this game are informative, not punitive: each one is the game telling you exactly which multiplier you've neglected.

# <span class="text-gray-900 dark:text-gray-100">The One Principle That Carries</span>

If you take a single idea from this section, take this: **progression in Slayer Legend is about multipliers, not increments.** A thousand extra ATK levels feel productive, but a promotion or a class grade multiplies everything you already have - every stat level, every piece of equipment, every buff. The players who advance fastest are the ones who always know what their next multiplier is and what's blocking it. Increments fill the time between multipliers; they aren't the plan itself.

And the boring corollary: the daily systems (training diary sessions, dungeon runs, ad scrolls, weekend Hot Time) are small individually and enormous in aggregate. Miss a promotion by a day and nothing is lost; skip your dailies for a month and you're measurably behind. Consistency beats intensity here - it's an idle game, after all. Play it like one.
