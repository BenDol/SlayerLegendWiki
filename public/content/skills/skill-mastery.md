# <span class="text-gray-900 dark:text-gray-100">Skill Mastery Overview</span>

Skill Mastery is mid-game content with late-game consequences: it unlocks **after clearing the Ether promotion** (recommended around level 1000, roughly stage 340 - the same milestone the community treats as the start of mid game). Once open, it adds a mastery layer over your whole skill kit: a points system you charge up through normal progression, and a spending tree that converts those points into permanent power - including the headline feature, **skill awakening**.

# <span class="text-gray-900 dark:text-gray-100">Earning Mastery Points</span>

Mastery points accrue from strengthening the things you were already strengthening: **leveling skills, upgrading spirits, and raising companion passives** all feed the pool. There's a pleasant design honesty to this - the game isn't asking for a new grind, it's paying you retroactively for the old ones. It does mean your mastery income is roughly proportional to how developed your account is, so players who kept their [spirits](/spirits) and [companion](/companions) passives current arrive at Ether with a head start.

# <span class="text-gray-900 dark:text-gray-100">Spending Points</span>

The mastery tree offers the fundamentals - **attack power, HP, extra gold, extra EXP** - plus the reason everyone actually cares about the system: **skill awakening**. The stat nodes are straightforward value and worth filling as points allow (extra gold and EXP nodes, in particular, quietly compound across every future hour of farming). But awakening is the star.

# <span class="text-gray-900 dark:text-gray-100">Skill Awakening</span>

Awakening doesn't just add a number to a skill - it **transforms** it, with significantly increased damage and, in many cases, changed effects. An awakened skill can behave like a new, better skill wearing the old one's name. Two mechanics worth knowing before you commit:

- Your **first awakening** of a skill spends mastery points.
- From the **second awakening onward**, the cost changes shape: instead of consuming points, you awaken by **winning a battle with the skill equipped**. The game asks you to prove the build, not just fund it.

That equip requirement has a real build implication - the skill you want to awaken has to earn a slot on your active bar for the awakening fight, so awakening plans and loadout plans are the same plan. Awaken the skills your build actually runs (your farming core and your boss burst), not whatever happens to be shiny.

# <span class="text-gray-900 dark:text-gray-100">Practical Advice</span>

If you're approaching Ether now: don't hoard toward some imagined perfect spend, but don't spray points randomly either. Fill cheap stat nodes for the compounding economy bonuses, then direct awakening at the one or two skills that headline your build - for most players that's the farming workhorse first (the Fulgurous-class picks), boss burst second. And keep leveling spirits and companion passives on schedule, since that's your point income.
