# <span class="text-gray-900 dark:text-gray-100">Soul Weapons Overview</span>

Soul weapons are the second blade your Slayer carries - crafted rather than summoned, wielded **alongside** your normal [weapon](/equipment/weapons), not instead of it. The currency is souls:<img src="/images/content/goods/Goods_SoulWhite.png" alt="" class="inline-image" style="display: inline-block; vertical-align: middle;width: 30px; height: 30px; margin: -2px 0 0 0;" data-inline="true" />**White**,<img src="/images/content/goods/Goods_SoulGreen.png" alt="" class="inline-image" style="display: inline-block; vertical-align: middle;width: 30px; height: 30px; margin: -2px 0 0 0;" data-inline="true" />**Green**,<img src="/images/content/goods/Goods_SoulRed.png" alt="" class="inline-image" style="display: inline-block; vertical-align: middle;width: 30px; height: 30px; margin: -2px 0 0 0;" data-inline="true" />**Red**, and<img src="/images/content/goods/Goods_SoulBlue.png" alt="" class="inline-image" style="display: inline-block; vertical-align: middle;width: 30px; height: 30px; margin: -2px 0 0 0;" data-inline="true" />**Blue** - harvested from the monsters you're already killing. White souls even accumulate while you idle offline, so the system quietly charges itself between sessions.

There are **81 soul weapons** in the wiki's current dataset, each with evocative names - Innocence, Coolness, Desire, Effort, Patience, Hatred, and on through Pride, Bravery, Wisdom, and beyond. Each weapon adds its ATK to your kit, belongs to one of three soul colors (Green, Red, or Blue), and gates behind a **stage requirement** - the very first, **Innocence**, becomes craftable in the Black Forest region (stages 61-80) for 2,000 souls. From there the ladder climbs with your stage progress for the rest of the game.

# <span class="text-gray-900 dark:text-gray-100">Crafting and Disassembly</span>

Soul weapons don't enhance the way normal gear does - you craft them, and their power comes from which ones you've made and what you do with them afterward. Made a weapon you no longer need? **Disassembly** returns a portion of the souls spent (half, on the early weapons), so soul investment is never fully sunk. The colors matter beyond flavor too: the [companions](/companions) each carry a late-unlocking skill that boosts the **completion effect** of one soul color's weapons - Ellie for Green, Miho for Red, Luna for Blue - one of several places the game rewards keeping systems aligned.

# <span class="text-gray-900 dark:text-gray-100">Engraving: The Grid Game</span>

At **stage 320**, when you reach the soul weapon **Pride**, the engraving system opens - and it's a genuinely different mini-game. Soul weapons gain a grid, and you fill it with tetris-style **soul gem pieces** that drop as you play. The mechanics:

- Seven piece shapes, each tied to a stat: the L-shape carries **ATK**, reverse-L carries **HP**, T-shape **HP Recovery**, square **Critical Damage**, line **Extra Gold**, and the two squiggles **Accuracy** and **Dodge**.
- Pieces come in six rarities, Common through Mythic, and can be **rotated** to fit.
- Fill every slot with no gaps and the weapon grants an extra **completion bonus** - the thing those companion skills amplify.
- <img src="/images/content/goods/Goods_ChaosSoul.png" alt="" class="inline-image" style="display: inline-block; vertical-align: middle;width: 30px; height: 30px; margin: -2px 0 0 0;" data-inline="true" />**Chaos Souls** reroll pieces into random new shapes and rarities, and higher soul weapon levels improve the odds of high-rarity pieces.

Priorities, if you want them: ATK pieces first, Critical Damage second, then survivability, with Extra Gold pieces earning a spot in dedicated farming setups. But completion bonuses reward clean fits over greedy stats, so the real skill is solving the grid - which is exactly why this wiki built the **[Soul Weapon Engraving tool](/soul-weapon-engraving)**. Lay out your grid there, experiment freely, and share layouts with other players instead of burning Chaos Souls on experiments in-game.

# <span class="text-gray-900 dark:text-gray-100">Practical Notes</span>

- Souls arrive on their own; you mostly just spend them on schedule as stage requirements unlock new crafts.
- Colored souls come from deeper stages, white souls from everywhere (and offline) - if a craft is soul-starved, the answer is usually "push further," not "farm longer."
- Engraving pieces are a long-haul collection. Reroll with Chaos Souls when a grid is close to completion, not as a slot machine.
