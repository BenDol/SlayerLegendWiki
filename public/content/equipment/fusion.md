# <span class="text-gray-900 dark:text-gray-100">Fusion Overview</span>

Fusion is the game's answer to bad summon luck: **five items of the same grade combine into one item of the next grade up.** It applies to [weapons](/equipment/weapons), [accessories](/equipment/accessories), and even [classes](/character/class) (five same-grade classes fuse to the next grade, which is exactly how you climb toward the top of the class ladder between lucky pulls). The rule is beautifully simple and it converts volume into progress - every duplicate that drops or summons is a fifth of something better.

# <span class="text-gray-900 dark:text-gray-100">Why Fusion Matters So Much</span>

The rarity ladders in this game are long - seven tiers for equipment, most carrying four sub-grades apiece, and twenty grades for classes - and summon odds get thin fast at the top (the class table is public: the best summonable class sits at a 0.01% rate). Nobody climbs those ladders on raw luck. Fusion is the deterministic path: enough summons at any grade *mathematically becomes* the grade above, and the grade above that. When veterans say weapon summons are the best diamond spend in the game, fusion is half the reason - no pull is ever truly wasted, because five of anything is one of the next thing.

The five-to-one ratio also means grades are exponentially expensive in raw material: one item two grades up costs twenty-five duplicates, three grades costs a hundred twenty-five. That exponential is why the ladder feels fast early and slow late, and why drops that feel worthless still matter. Feed the machine.

# <span class="text-gray-900 dark:text-gray-100">Fusing Without Regrets</span>

A few things worth knowing before you hold the button down:

- **Owned effects are the tradeoff.** Equipment grants a passive owned bonus just for being in your collection, and fusing consumes the copies. In practice the fused result is nearly always worth more than five duplicates' owned effects - but it's the reason to fuse *duplicates* first and think twice before feeding unique pieces you only hold one of.
- **Check skill requirements.** Some [skills](/skills) are learned by consuming specific weapons or accessories. Fusing away a piece a skill needs is the classic avoidable mistake - if a piece looks oddly specific, check before it goes in the blender.
- **Don't fuse your equipped piece's backups blindly.** If your current weapon dies to an unlucky streak of "almost enough for the next grade," you want a wearable fallback. Cheap insurance.
- **Batch it.** Fusion is most satisfying (and least error-prone) done in deliberate passes - after a summon session, sort, fuse the obvious duplicates, and stop when choices stop being obvious.

# <span class="text-gray-900 dark:text-gray-100">Fusion vs. Enhancement</span>

The two systems answer different questions. [Enhancement](/equipment/enhancement) makes the item you have stronger, up to its level cap; fusion replaces it with a categorically better one. Mid-game rhythm is usually: enhance your equipped gear to a comfortable level, keep fusing toward the next grade in the background, and when the fusion lands, swap and repeat. Cubes and copies, alternating. That loop, run patiently for weeks, is the entire equipment game - and honestly, watching five swords become one better sword never completely stops being satisfying.
