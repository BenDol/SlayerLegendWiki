# <span class="text-gray-900 dark:text-gray-100">Accessories Overview</span>

Accessories are your second equipment slot, running on the same machinery as [weapons](/equipment/weapons): the same **Common through Immortal** rarity ladder with Grade 4 to Grade 1 sub-grades, the same dual **equip effect / owned effect** structure, the same [enhancement](/equipment/enhancement) to level 200 with<img src="/images/content/goods/EnchantCube 1_1.png" alt="" class="inline-image" style="display: inline-block; vertical-align: middle;width: 30px; height: 30px; margin: -2px 0 0 0;" data-inline="true" />**Enhance Cubes**, and the same five-into-one [fusion](/equipment/fusion). Where weapons drive your ATK, accessories are the game's primary source of **HP and HP Recovery** - every accessory carries them, and the higher tiers layer utility on top: from Rare grade upward accessories add **mana recovery** as an owned effect, and Mythic and above add **Extra EXP and Total Mana** as well. Survival plus economy, in other words - which shapes how urgently they deserve your resources (spoiler: less urgently than you'd think).

# <span class="text-gray-900 dark:text-gray-100">Getting Accessories</span>

- **Stage drops.** Accessories actually drop more generously than weapons in the early stages, starting at Common grades and scaling up to Legendary-grade drops around the stage 500 range. As with weapons, drops feed your owned-effect collection and fusion pipeline all game.
- **Summoning.** The shop's accessory summon works like the weapon summon, diamonds in, with the pool improving as your summon level climbs.

# <span class="text-gray-900 dark:text-gray-100">When to Invest</span>

Here's the honest advice, and it's the same advice the community's veteran guides give: **don't pour early diamonds into accessories.** Weapons first, classes next, spirits after that - accessories sit fourth in the queue. The reasoning is simple math: your damage pushes stages, stages pay for everything else, and accessories don't push damage the way weapons do. A solid Legendary Grade 1 accessory, once you land one, carries you a very long way - many players ride one deep into late game without regret, and enhancing past "good enough" has real opportunity cost while your weapon still has room to grow.

The exception is when survivability is the actual wall - some promotion fights and dungeon bosses hit hard enough that HP and recovery stop being optional. When you find yourself dying rather than running out of damage, that's your signal the accessory slot has been neglected.

# <span class="text-gray-900 dark:text-gray-100">The Endgame Accessory</span>

At the very top of the ladder sits the Immortal accessory, the **Orb of Hope** - a monster of an item that opens at three million HP and HP Recovery at enhancement level 1, and, like the final weapon, can be enhanced beyond the level 200 cap that binds everything below it. The Orb of Hope also matters beyond its own stats: **awakening** it produces the black pieces used to awaken [Black Orb](/equipment/black-orbs) equipment, tying the accessory line directly into the late-game elemental system. If you're at that stage of the game, the Black Orb page covers how the pieces (literally) fit together.

# <span class="text-gray-900 dark:text-gray-100">Practical Notes</span>

- Keep every accessory until you understand owned effects - your collection is passive stats, and even fusion should chew through duplicates before uniques.
- Accessory enhancement uses the same cube budget as your weapon. When cubes are tight (they always are), the weapon wins.
- Skills can require specific equipment to learn, so an accessory sitting "useless" in your inventory occasionally turns out to be a key. Another reason hoarding is correct in this game.

For planning a full setup around your accessory, the [Battle Loadouts](/battle-loadouts) tool has you covered.
