# <span class="text-gray-900 dark:text-gray-100">Enhancement Overview</span>

Owning good gear is half the job; the other half is leveling it. Enhancement pumps<img src="/images/content/goods/EnchantCube 1_1.png" alt="" class="inline-image" style="display: inline-block; vertical-align: middle;width: 30px; height: 30px; margin: -2px 0 0 0;" data-inline="true" />**Enhance Cubes** into a piece of equipment to raise its level and multiply its stat contribution. It applies across the board - [weapons](/equipment/weapons), [accessories](/equipment/accessories), and your [class](/character/class) all drink from the same cube supply, which makes cubes one of the most quietly contested resources in the game. There is never a point where you have enough of them. Plan accordingly.

# <span class="text-gray-900 dark:text-gray-100">The Level 200 Cap</span>

Standard equipment enhances to a maximum of **level 200**. The exceptions are at the very top of the ladder: the final Immortal weapon (**Orr**) and final accessory (**Orb of Hope**) can push beyond it - veteran players show these enhanced well past 300. Classes follow their own version of the same idea: a base cap of 200, raised by +50 per class awakening. Costs scale as levels climb, so the last stretch to a cap always costs dramatically more than the road that got you there. Standard idle-game rules apply - the cheap early levels on a new piece are always better value than the expensive last levels on an old one, right up until you're keeping a piece forever.

# <span class="text-gray-900 dark:text-gray-100">What to Enhance First</span>

A simple priority that holds for most of the game:

1. **Your equipped weapon.** It's your damage multiplier; every cube here pushes stages, and stages pay for everything else.
2. **Your class.** Once unlocked at stage 120, class enhancement is a permanent account-wide multiplier and shares the cube budget.
3. **Your equipped accessory.** Level it when survivability is the wall, not before. "Good enough" is a real tier for accessories.

And the caution that saves people pain: **think before dumping cubes into gear you're about to replace.** If your weapon is one fusion away from the next grade, those cubes may serve you better waiting for the upgrade. A replaced piece still contributes its owned effect from your collection, but your equipped multiplier is what wins fights - keep the cubes pointed at the piece you'll actually be wearing next week.

# <span class="text-gray-900 dark:text-gray-100">Where Cubes Come From</span>

- **Stage farming and idle rewards** - the baseline drip, scaling with your stage. Notably, cube income holds up well offline, so overnight idling is genuinely productive for enhancement even when active play is off the table.
- **The Closed Mine dungeon** - the dedicated cube source, entered with your daily<img src="/images/content/goods/Goods_Wing.png" alt="" class="inline-image" style="display: inline-block; vertical-align: middle;width: 30px; height: 30px; margin: -5px 0 0 0;" data-inline="true" />**Glow Feathers** and boosted further by ad scrolls. Ten feathers a day, every day - the players who never skip the Mine simply out-enhance the players who do.
- **Events and quest rewards** - periodic top-ups worth claiming but not planning around.

# <span class="text-gray-900 dark:text-gray-100">Related Systems</span>

Enhancement raises levels within a grade; [fusion](/equipment/fusion) is how you jump grades entirely - the two work together, cube-leveling what you own while fusing toward what's next. And once mythic-grade equipment enters the picture, [Black Orb awakening](/equipment/black-orbs) adds a further layer on top of enhanced gear. One system at a time, though. The cube grind comes first, and it never really ends... it just gets bigger numbers.
