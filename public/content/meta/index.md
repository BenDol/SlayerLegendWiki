# <span class="text-gray-900 dark:text-gray-100">About the Wiki Itself</span>

Everything in this section is about the wiki rather than the game: who maintains it, how to reach them, how editing works, and what the site does with your data. If you are here to learn Slayer Legend, the [Getting Started](/getting-started) section is the place; if you are here to help run the wiki, or want to know who is behind it before you trust a number, read on.

# <span class="text-gray-900 dark:text-gray-100">The Pages</span>

**[About](/meta/about)** explains what the wiki is, who writes and reviews it, where its data comes from, and its relationship to the game's developer (it is an independent fan project). **[Contact](/meta/contact)** lists the ways to reach the maintainers - the community Discord, GitHub issues, and email - and what each channel is best for. **[Contributing](/meta/contributing)** walks through editing a page: signing in with GitHub or editing anonymously, how the review step works, and what happens to your change after you submit it. **[Guidelines](/meta/guidelines)** is the style and formatting reference for editors, including the wiki's custom syntax for embedding skill, equipment and data previews. **[Emoticons](/meta/emoticons)** catalogues the game's emoticon set and how to use it in a page. **[Privacy Policy](/meta/privacy-policy)** covers cookies, analytics, advertising and the account data the wiki stores.

# <span class="text-gray-900 dark:text-gray-100">How Editing Works, in Short</span>

Every page on the wiki is a markdown file in a public repository. An edit made through the site becomes a proposed change that a maintainer reviews before it publishes; nothing lands on a page unreviewed, which is what lets the wiki stay open to anyone while keeping the reference trustworthy. The full history of every page is public, so you can always see who changed a value and when. Contributors are credited on the [highscore](/highscore), and the most active earn a star next to their name across the site.

# <span class="text-gray-900 dark:text-gray-100">If Something Is Wrong</span>

A wrong number is the most useful thing you can report. The fastest route is to [edit the page](/meta/contributing) yourself with the value you see in-game; if you would rather not, the [Contact](/meta/contact) page explains how to tell the maintainers. Problems with the site itself - a broken tool, a page that will not load - belong in GitHub issues, where they are tracked publicly until fixed.
